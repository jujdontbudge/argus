The dispatch prompt was also checked byte-for-byte against the registry's
copy, because a prompt that differs by one byte misses the dispatch hash and
classifies as JOHAN — the USER.md poisoning path reached through a quoting bug:

  expected 1212 bytes / delivered 1212 bytes / BYTE-IDENTICAL: true

This is not free, and the mechanism is worth writing down. `cmux send --help`
documents "Escape sequences: \n and \r send Enter, \t sends Tab", and
`shellQuote` escapes exactly those three characters to keep the launch line on
one physical line. Every one of them is re-expanded by the transport back into
the keystroke it was escaping, so the line arrives in ~20 pieces and zsh sits
in `quote>` continuation between them. It survives because a real newline
inside `$'…'` IS a literal newline: the reassembled argv is identical. Measured
here, not reasoned about — but it survives by accident, not by design, and
`launch.ts` says its shape is "asserted against a real shell in
launch.test.ts": that test asserts against a real shell and never against a
real cmux, which is where the behaviour lives.
