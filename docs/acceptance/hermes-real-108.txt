3. IS `HERMES_HOME`-AT-SCRATCH A REAL DRIVE, OR THE SCRATCH HALF RENAMED?
    ==> IT IS THE SCRATCH HALF, AND NOT EVEN RENAMED. IT IS LITERALLY IT.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This was the whole question, and it has a flat answer.

  (a) The scratch half ALREADY IS "HERMES_HOME at scratch". `hermesDriveEnv`
      (hermes-live-gate.ts) sets, unconditionally and last so no ambient value
      can win:
          env.HERMES_HOME = drive.hermesHome    <- the scratch profile directory
          env.HOME        = drive.home          <- the scratch home
      There is no second thing for a "HERMES_HOME at scratch" run to be.

  (b) HERMES_HOME CANNOT MOVE THE `real` HALF AT ALL. `REAL_HERMES_ROOT` is
      `join(homedir(), ".hermes")`; the env var is never consulted. MEASURED,
      with HERMES_HOME pointed at a tree that DOES contain a qualifying profile:

        HERMES_HOME env             /private/tmp/t108-hermes/probe-real
        HOME env                    /Users/johansardar
        os.homedir()                /Users/johansardar
        REAL_HERMES_ROOT            /Users/johansardar/.hermes
        probe.realProfile           undefined
        gate.real                   not-run
        findRealProfile(that tree)  <tree>/profiles/argus-real-108/state.db

      The last two lines together are the proof: the profile is right there and
      findRealProfile finds it when asked directly — and the GATE still says
      not-run, because the gate never asks about HERMES_HOME.

  (c) THE ONE LEVER THAT DOES MOVE IT IS `HOME`, AND USING IT WOULD BE A
      MANUFACTURED PASS. `os.homedir()` honours $HOME. MEASURED:

        HOME env                    /private/tmp/t108-hermes/fakehome

      Point HOME at a directory you assembled yourself and the `real` half goes
      GREEN. THIS IS RECORDED HERE SO THAT NOBODY DISCOVERS IT LATER AND THINKS
      IT IS THE ANSWER. It is not evidence and #108 REFUSES IT: the half's claim

4. WHAT COULD BE RUN, AND WAS: THE CLAIM UNDER THE HALF, AGAINST REAL BYTES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The half's TITLE names two claims, and they are separable:

  "a deployed Hermes persists an assistant row  |  the read-only source can tail"
   ^-- deployment shape, needs the real home        ^-- reader contract, needs
       and is unrunnable (§1)                           only real Hermes bytes

The second is reachable without writing anything, and this ticket ran it.
Johan's daily-driver ~/.hermes/state.db is a real Hermes database, written by
the SAME installed v0.16.0 from turns a real provider really answered. It is
not in `profiles/` and is not `argus-*`, so the production source will never
find it in place — `createHermesSource` only ever globs `<root>/profiles/argus-*`

  A MEASUREMENT FALLING OUT OF THIS, WORTH ITS OWN LINE: the first attempt

  The real database is in WAL mode. A `mode=ro` open of a WAL database with no
  companion `-shm` cannot proceed, because SQLite would have to CREATE that
  `-shm` first. So `createHermesSource` pointed at the real ~/.hermes in place
  would either fail outright or make SQLite write into the real tree. This is a
  THIRD independent reason the half must not be run in situ, and it is one the
  house rule did not have to supply.

WHAT §4 ESTABLISHES:  research §7's caveat in its POSITIVE form. A real Hermes
  DOES persist assistant rows — 45,295 of them — and the unmodified production
  reader and parser turn them into MessageEvents with role='assistant', with
  session-meta stitched on and the model resolved. The reader's SQL, its column
  names, its schema line and its role filter all hold against real v0.16.0
  output at schema 15.
WHAT §4 DOES NOT ESTABLISH:  the deployment shape. It says nothing about whether
  `argus up`'s binding leaves a profile the source discovers in the real home,
  because the clone's location was chosen by this probe, not by a deployment.
  §2's scratch drive is what covers the binding, and it covers it against a
  scratch profile.

6. THE DECISION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`ARGUS_LIVE_HERMES`'s `real` half is a PERMANENT STRUCTURAL LIMIT ON THIS
MACHINE. It is not a to-do, not a gap, and not something a future audit should
re-open. Four independent reasons, each sufficient:

  1. Its precondition is a DIRECTORY, and every route that creates it writes the
     real ~/.hermes — including a copy of a 484-line credential-bearing `.env`
     and, via `profile create`, an executable dropped into ~/.local/bin (§1).
  2. There is no read-only `hermes` subcommand; even one that fails argparse
     creates two log files under its HERMES_HOME (§1).
  3. The production reader cannot open the real WAL-mode database read-only
     without SQLite creating a `-shm` in the real tree (§4).
  4. The real tree is under continuous concurrent write by a live gateway, so
     nothing measured over it would be reproducible anyway (§5).

`HERMES_HOME` cannot relax this: it does not reach the `real` half at all (§3b).
`HOME` can, and doing so would be a manufactured pass, refused (§3c).

WHICH SPEC CLAIM IS LEFT UNVERIFIABLE — exactly one, and it is narrow:

  That `argus up`'s Hermes binding, run against the REAL ~/.hermes, produces a
  profile the production source discovers and tails in place.

  Not "Hermes persists assistant rows" — §4 settles that against 45,295 real
  rows.  Not "the source reads them" — §4 settles that through the unmodified
  reader.  Not "the generated profile name is valid and selectable" — #102
  settled that against the real binary.  Not "the binding produces a profile
  Hermes accepts and bootstraps a state.db in" — §2's scratch drive settles
  that, and #47 already argued the profile "is not a directory invented for the
  test; it is the one convergeAgentBindings creates on a real deployment".

  What is left is ONLY the substitution of the real root for a scratch root in
  that binding — a path prefix. The code is identical; `hermesRoot` is a
  parameter (`src/up/bindings.ts:733`). So the residue is: does anything about
  the REAL tree specifically (its existing config.yaml, its 484-line .env, its
  live gateway, the fact that `profiles/` must be created rather than found)
  make the binding behave differently than it does against an empty scratch
  root? That is a real question and this ticket cannot answer it.

IS IT COVERED IN SUBSTANCE? Largely, by three neighbours, and the gap that
remains should be stated plainly rather than rounded to zero:

  §2 / #47's scratch half   the binding, the profile Hermes accepts, the
                            state.db it bootstraps, and §7's caveat in its
                            falsifiable negative form — against a scratch root.
  #102                      the generated name against the real `hermes` binary:
                            listed, selected, memories symlink resolving. This
                            is the closest anything has come to the real tree
                            and it stopped at the same wall.
  §4 / this ticket          §7's caveat in its POSITIVE form against real bytes,
                            and #100's shape contract holding at schema 15.
  #99's redrive             a Hermes drive at the orchestrator level.

  NOT covered: the real-root binding itself. The honest grade is "covered except
  for a path prefix, and that prefix is exactly what the house rule forbids".

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE FOR IT TO BE RUNNABLE — three options, in the order a
future reader should consider them:

  1. IN THE HOUSE RULES (the only realistic one, and still probably not worth
     it). Johan deploys Argus on a real repo of his own, for his own reasons.
     Then `~/.hermes/profiles/argus-<repo>` exists because he wanted it to, the
     half's precondition is met by his usage rather than by a test, and the half
     "starts running by itself" exactly as #47 predicted. Nothing in Argus needs
     to change; the gate is already written to notice. THE RIGHT POSTURE IS TO
     WAIT FOR THAT, NOT TO ENGINEER IT.
  2. IN ARGUS. Make `REAL_HERMES_ROOT` injectable rather than
     `join(homedir(), ".hermes")` — but do NOT do this. It converts a limit into
     a knob, and §3(c) shows the knob's only use is turning the half green
     against a tree the caller built. The hardcoding is load-bearing.
  3. IN HERMES. A genuinely read-only mode — no logs, no `active_profile`, no
     `-shm` — plus a profile that could be created outside the home. Neither
     exists at v0.16.0 and neither is Argus's to add.

WHAT #108 REFUSED TO DO
  - run `hermes profile use` against the real home (#102's refusal, upheld and
    now measured: it writes `<HERMES_HOME>/active_profile`)
  - run `argus up`, `hermes profile create`, or any other write against the real
    ~/.hermes
  - point `HOME` at a scratch tree to make the `real` half report `run` (§3c)
  - point `createHermesSource` at the real ~/.hermes in place (§4, WAL/-shm)
  - open or copy ~/.hermes/.env or ~/.hermes/auth.json for content
  - print any line of Johan's real transcripts; they are counted, never quoted
