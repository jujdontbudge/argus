4. WHY `leaseTakeover` FAILED, AND WHAT IT ACTUALLY MEASURED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The check (analyze117.ts:348-356) asks whether the replacement pid appears as
  `health.json`'s `pid` in some SAMPLE within LEASE_TAKEOVER_MS = 120,000 ms of
  the restart. It reads `sample.repos[i].health[eye].pid` — the watcher's own
  written health file, not the supervisor's status claim. That is the right
  field to ask about, and the check is well motivated: the docblock explains
  that a replacement which cannot take the `watch.lock` returns 0 from
  `runLoop` before writing any health while status still lists it as running.

  41 of the 43 restarts took the lease over. Measured delta from the
  `restarted` event to the first sample carrying that pid in health:

    minimum   0.1 s   (idx 17, digest)
    maximum  15.2 s   (idx  4, archiver)
    median    ~6 s

  Note the shape of that distribution: the maximum, 15.2 s, is one sampler
  interval. Takeover latency is bounded ABOVE by the observation cadence, which
  is what "too fast to measure with this instrument" looks like.

  The two failures are the two pids the sampler never had an opportunity to
  see. Both are in the brake phase, on `retro`, and both are explained without
  reference to any lease behaviour at all:

  idx 37 — pid 65148. Restarted 17:58:56.711, killed again 17:59:04.946.
           Total lifetime 8.2 seconds.
           Bracketing samples: 17:58:55.181 and 17:59:11.143.
           The pid was born 1.5 s AFTER one sample and killed 6.2 s BEFORE the
           next. Its entire life fell strictly between two consecutive samples.
           There was no sample in which it COULD have been observed.

           This is structural, not bad luck: the brake phase kills the same eye
           roughly every 8-10 s in order to force five restarts inside a
           five-minute window, while the sampler runs at 15 s. The scenario is
           designed to produce replacements shorter-lived than the instrument's
           resolution.

  idx 40 — pid 66591. Restarted 17:59:35.237, first seen in health at
           18:02:56.946 — a delta of 201.7 s, over the 120 s bar.
           The sample that finally saw it reports intervalMs = 210,193: it is
           suspend-gap candidate #5 in the tool's OWN reportedNotJudged list.
           The sampler was not running between 17:59:26.753 and 18:02:56.946.
           The pid was observed at the FIRST opportunity the sampler had.

  So neither failure is a watcher that failed to take a lease. One is a pid
  that lived entirely between two samples; the other is a pid first observable
  only after a 3.5-minute sampler blackout. In both cases the harness saw
  nothing and scored "nothing" as "failure".

  There is a sharper way to put the second one. analyze117.ts's docblock states
  that the suspendGaps section "is excluded from the overall verdict by
  construction" because a run in which the machine never slept must not be
  scored for a property it never tested. But the very gaps it detects as
  suspend candidates are what pushed idx 40 past its 120 s bar and turned the
  overall verdict to FAIL. Sleep/wake is therefore scored into the verdict
  after all, through the back door, in the FAIL direction — the exact thing the
  docblock says it refuses to do. That is filed as H1 in §6.

6. HARNESS DEFECTS — REPORTED, NOT PATCHED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Same discipline as #116 §5b. No sealed script was edited. `git status` at
  commit time shows only this evidence file staged.

  H1  analyze117.ts:348-356, `healthPidSeen` / `leaseTakeover`.
      The function returns a bare `false` when no sample in the window carries
      the pid, and `leaseTakeover` treats `false` as a failure. There is no
      third state for "the window contained no usable observation". The file is
      otherwise careful about exactly this: `inBudget` and `leaseTakenOver` are
      set to `null` when `newPid` is null, and `leaseJudged` filters nulls out.
      The same nulling is needed when the sampler produced no sample covering
      the replacement's lifetime.
      Impact on THIS run: 2 of 43 restarts, both false negatives, and they are
      the sole cause of one of the two failing components.
      Narrower consequence: because the brake phase kills faster than the
      sampler samples, this check can never be reliable in the brake phase as
      currently parameterised, whatever the code under test does.

  H2  analyze117.ts, `bystander`.
      Counts supervisor restart lines across the whole log with no teardown
      window excluded, while `noMessageLoss` in the same file excludes the last
      120 s by design and says why. Impact on this run: 1 line, and it is the
      sole cause of the second failing component.

  H3  corrupt117.ts:121-151, `corrupt()`.
      The docblock describes `page-garbage` as overwriting "4 KiB in the MIDDLE
      of the main database file" and `truncate-half` as cutting "the main
      database file to half its length". The archiver runs `PRAGMA
      journal_mode = WAL` (src/eyes/archiver/archiver.ts:62), and the scenario
      corrupts after only SETTLE_MS = 25 s, at which point nothing has been
      checkpointed: `archive.db` is a 4,096-byte one-page file and all 25 rows
      live in a 737,512-byte WAL. The run recorded this itself:

        page-garbage : {"applied":true,"bytes":4096,"dbBytesBefore":4096,
                        "offset":4096,"target":"main-db"}
        truncate-half: {"applied":true,"dbBytesBefore":4096,"dbBytesAfter":2048,
                        "target":"main-db"}

      For page-garbage the offset is computed as
        Math.max(4096, Math.floor(size / 2 / 4096) * 4096)
      With size = 4096 this is Math.max(4096, 0) = 4096 — exactly EOF. The
      guard that exists to avoid clobbering page 1 silently converts "middle of
      the file" into "append a new page past the end" for any database of two
      pages or fewer, and SQLite takes its page count from the header, so a
      trailing page is ignored entirely. Nothing in the live database was
      touched.
      For truncate-half, a 4,096 -> 2,048 byte cut destroys the header of a
      file that contained no rows, while every row was in the WAL and the live
      archiver held the database open.
      Consequence: two of the three corruption modes were no-ops. See §7.

  H4  diskfull117.sh, the `rows()` helper and the integrity-check invocation.
      Both call `bun -e '<code>' "<path>"` and read `process.argv[2]`. Under
      `bun -e` the argument lands at `argv[1]`:

        $ bun -e 'console.log(JSON.stringify(process.argv))' /tmp/somepath.db
        ["/Users/johansardar/.bun/bin/bun","/tmp/somepath.db"]

      `argv[2]` is undefined, `new Database(undefined, {readonly:true})` opens
      an anonymous database, and that combination throws. Every row count in
      `diskfull-result.json`, every line of `full-timeline.txt` and
      `recovery-timeline.txt`, and `integrityAfter`, are all the same string:
      "ERR Cannot open an anonymous database in read-only mode." See §8 for
      what was recovered instead.

  None of H1-H4 were fixed here. H1 and H2 change a PASS/FAIL verdict and
  should be decided by the dispatcher, not by the worker whose run they judge.
  H3 and H4 are in scripts committed pre-registered in 07a392e and are reported
  under the same rule.

  archiver was probably never denied a write. A 20 MiB volume minus 18 MiB of

  archiver produced no crash, no stderr flood, no memory growth and no lost

  so the killer left its loop because the deadline passed, not because it died,

9c. The 401s, and whether the scratch was deliberately credential-degraded.

  DELIBERATE, and it matches #109's honcho pattern. run117.sh builds a scratch
  HOME and points every credential root at it:
    export HOME="$HOME_DIR"
    export CODEX_HOME="$HOME_DIR/.codex"
    export HERMES_HOME="$HOME_DIR/.hermes"
  and copies no credentials into any of them. Verified: `find <scratch>/home
  -name auth.json` returns nothing, and <scratch>/home/.codex contains no
  auth.json. Real credential checksums were unchanged throughout (§1). This is
  a scratch running fully credential-degraded — not an architecture violation
  and not a leak.

  WHAT IT LIMITS. It means #117 cannot claim "retro recovers its BRAIN function
  after a kill", because retro's brain could not succeed in this environment
  whether or not it was killed. It does NOT limit the restart claims:
  restartBudget, restartCompletion, brake and leaseTakeover all read process
  liveness, supervisor stderr and health.json's `pid` field, none of which
  depend on a brain call succeeding. "Does the process come back, take its
  lease, and stay alive" is answered; "does the eye resume doing useful
  cognitive work" is not, for the four brain-backed eyes.

9d. Does analyze117.ts conflate these with chaos failures? NO.

  Checked deliberately, because conflation would make the verdict meaningless.
  None of components 1-7 read health.lastError or health.lastSuccess;
  leaseTakeover reads only health[eye].pid. The oversized-input and 401
  failures therefore did not contribute to the FAIL. That is a point in the
  sealed script's favour and is recorded as such.
  The corresponding limitation: the script consequently CANNOT detect a
  watcher that returns, takes its lease, and then does no useful work. That
  precise failure — a resumed watcher archiving nothing while reporting
  backlog 0 and no error — is the bug 41cbcf5 fixed, and it was caught by the
  sentinel set-subtraction in noMessageLoss, not by any health check.

  chaos was not observed; only the deliberately-forced case was. The unit-level
  property is separately covered by
  src/supervisor/runtime.test.ts "backs off exponentially and gives up after

  name explicitly if deferred". It is deferred, and named here. Nothing in
  run117.sh, killer117.ts, corrupt117.ts or diskfull117.sh manipulates any

  SLEEP/WAKE (item 3) — INSTRUMENTED, NOT SCORED, AS DESIGNED — but see §4.

  DISK-FULL LOUDNESS — OPEN. Per §8, the volume filled but the archiver was

  is not.

  MULTI-REPO CHAOS AT N>2 — out of scope here. This run was N=2 by design
